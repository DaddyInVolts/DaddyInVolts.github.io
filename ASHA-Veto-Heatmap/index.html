ASHA Veto Heatmap

Showing which experiments help or hurt each quality veto — at a glance
Public design demo — fictional data. Both prototypes run on randomly generated numbers, and the experiment names, products and organisations shown are invented for this demo. They exist to test the interaction design, not to report any result. The veto structure is representative of an ASHA-style guardrail model.

The problem

Quality guardrails — the "vetoes" that fire when a user hits a failed boot, a crash, a blank screen, a throttle — are measured across a dozen-plus separate metrics grouped into three pillars. Existing metric-impact views answer "which experiments moved this one metric?", one metric at a time. Answering the real question — "across our whole veto set, where are we winning and where are we bleeding?" — means paging through 18 separate views and holding the comparison in your head.

The idea

A single heatmap: experiments down the side, all 18 vetoes across the top, grouped by pillar. Every cell is a square where

  • size encodes the magnitude of net user impact, and
  • colour encodes direction.

Vetoes are desirable-down, so more vetoes is worse:

More vetoes — regression Inconclusive Fewer vetoes — improvement No movement

One glance shows both the experiments that are broadly damaging quality and the vetoes that are being hit from many directions at once. Clicking a cell narrows the page to that experiment; the ranked lists below then drill into the underlying detail.

Two takes

The same concept is shown twice, in two different host surfaces, to test which framing lands better with reviewers. Feedback especially welcome on: whether size-plus-colour is readable at 18 columns, whether the pillar grouping is the right cut, and whether the heatmap should lead the page or support the ranked lists.
