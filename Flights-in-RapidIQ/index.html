Section 1

Problem & Opportunity

Problem Statement

RapidIQ is IDEAS' modern self-serve analytics environment: PMs, DS, engineering, and business stakeholders build dashboards and insights without needing deep knowledge of datasets or pipelines. It supports UDM and Agile metrics, deep-links from AskIDEAS, and produces custom insights in minutes. What it does not yet expose is experimentation flight assignments — the mapping of users (or other assignment units) into the treatment and control groups of IDEAS flights and flight extensions. As a result, Growth PMs and DS today cannot use RapidIQ to (a) filter any RapidIQ dashboard to the population inside a specific flight or variant ("self-serve segmenting"), or (b) compute metric lifts across variants on the fly — including on the provisional and Agile metrics that RapidIQ already carries but that scorecards do not. To answer either question today, growth crews leave RapidIQ, request a bespoke analysis, and wait — often days — for someone to join flight-assignment data to user-level metric data by hand.

Opportunity

Onboarding IDEAS flights and flight extensions into RapidIQ unlocks two closely related capabilities for Growth crews in the same interface they already use for portfolio analytics:

  • Self-serve segmenting. Any RapidIQ dashboard can be filtered to the users assigned to a specific flight, variant, or extension — turning existing dashboards into flight-scoped views without a rebuild
  • On-the-fly experimentation insights. Growth crews can compute metric-level lifts (treatment vs control) inside RapidIQ, across the full RapidIQ metrics catalog — including provisional metrics that scorecards do not carry
  • Faster iteration. Insight cycles collapse from days (bespoke pulls) to minutes (self-serve), matching RapidIQ's core value proposition for the rest of the analytics surface area
  • Consistent flight semantics. A single canonical source for "who was in what variant when" avoids the drift that emerges when each crew joins flight data on their own

Evidence

  • Existing ADO commitment: Deliverable #7483279 "Experimentation in RapidIQ" (Area: IDEAs\Growth\Experimentation, State: New) captures customer demand: "Onboard IDEAS flights/flight extension to RapidIQ so users can self serve segments and experimentation insights. Especially powerful when RapidIQ has data that scorecards do not, like provisional data."
  • Provisional-metric coverage gap: Scorecards expose only the metric set they were built for; RapidIQ carries a broader, faster-moving catalog including Agile and provisional metrics. Growth crews consistently ask "is my experiment moving this RapidIQ metric?" and today have no self-serve path to answer it.
  • Adjacent RapidIQ investments already landing: RapidIQ Provisional Metrics integration (#7566969), RapidIQ WorkIQ metrics (#7469097), and the completed RapidIQ IGI Handshake (#7394747) show the platform is actively growing its catalog — experimentation is the missing dimension.
  • Known engineering signal: Comment from Kenny Long: "Large investment to bring in and join user level data in CH to calculate metrics on the fly in RapidIQ." This is a real cost driver and shapes the phasing of this work — see Open Questions.
  • Funding status: Not funded in FY27 Q1 (per PM comment); H1 direction is to drive clarity and unblock estimation.

Goals & Success Metrics

Goals

  1. Make IDEAS flight-assignment data (flight, flight extension, variant, assignment unit) available inside RapidIQ so any dashboard can be filtered by flight cohort — the "self-serve segmenting" capability
  2. Enable metric-level treatment-vs-control comparisons in RapidIQ across the full metric catalog, including provisional metrics — the "on-the-fly experimentation insights" capability

Section 3

User Scenarios

Scenario 1 — "Growth PM Self-Serving a Flight Segment" (Primary)

Priya is a Growth PM running a flight on the Copilot Home Page CTA. She wants to answer "how do the users in the treatment variant behave across our standard usage dashboard?" Today she requests a bespoke Kusto pull and waits a couple of days. With this spec shipped, Priya opens her regular RapidIQ dashboard, opens the filter panel, selects Flight = "CopilotHomeCTA-2026H1", Variant = Treatment, and every chart on the dashboard recomputes against that cohort. She toggles between Treatment and Control to eyeball the difference in engagement, session-length, and retention metrics — all within the same dashboard she already uses for portfolio monitoring.

Scenario 2 — "Data Scientist Computing Provisional Lifts"

Dana is a Data Scientist investigating whether a Copilot flight moves a provisional metric that isn't yet in the scorecard catalog. Today, the scorecard says nothing about it and she has to write a one-off pipeline. With this spec, she opens RapidIQ, adds the provisional metric to a dashboard, applies the flight filter, and switches to a "By variant" breakdown. She sees the treatment vs control lift on the provisional metric side-by-side with the established metrics, decides the signal warrants a real experiment scorecard extension, and files that request with a concrete lift estimate in hand.

Scenario 3 — "Growth Leader Reviewing Flight Behavior at Portfolio Level"

Jordan is a Growth org leader wanting to see how active flights are shifting core engagement metrics across the portfolio. Jordan opens the "Copilot Engagement" RapidIQ dashboard, applies a Flight filter to "Any active flight in area = Copilot Growth", and sees per-metric lift decomposition. Instead of aggregating scorecards after the fact, Jordan can see the current state of the experimentation portfolio inside the same dashboard everyone already trusts.

Edge Case — Metric Not Available at the Assignment Unit

A flight is assigned at the user level, but the metric under analysis is only defined at the tenant grain. The UI surfaces an inline warning: "This metric is defined at Tenant grain; the current flight is assigned at User grain. Treatment/control assignment will be inherited to the tenant level via a documented rule, and results may include tenant-level assignment collisions." The user can proceed with the caveat visible, or switch to a metric at the compatible grain.

Edge Case — Flight Still Ramping / SRM Detected

The flight has an SRM (sample-ratio mismatch) or is still ramping. Rather than silently displaying misleading lifts, RapidIQ tags the flight filter with a data-quality chip ("SRM detected", "Ramping — variant counts still growing") and any lift computations render with an explicit warning. Users can dismiss to proceed, matching the transparency posture ExP uses.

Section 5

Design Direction

Where the Feature Shows Up in RapidIQ

Left pane — Metrics catalog: new "Experimentation" tag alongside existing workload tags; flights surface in the catalog by area/owner/state.

Right pane — Filter & dimension panel: new "Flight" filter, dynamically populated from metadata (Flight, Extension, Variant); an "Assignment unit" chip clarifies grain.

Canvas — per-chart controls: a "By Variant" toggle on any metric that supports it; renders Treatment/Control side-by-side plus a lift chip.

Header — data-quality banner: SRM detected · Ramping · Freshness stale — matches existing RapidIQ banner style.

Interaction Notes

  • Flight filter follows RapidIQ's metadata-driven pattern — controls render from flight metadata; nothing is hard-coded
  • When a Flight filter is active, the dashboard header shows a chip: "Segmented by Flight: X · Variant: Treatment" with a one-click "Compare variants" action to switch to By-Variant view
  • By-Variant view exposes a per-metric lift chip; hover reveals the underlying counts, the assignment window used, and any caveats
  • Deep links carry the Flight filter as URL state so a shared link opens the exact view
  • Where a metric's grain doesn't match the flight's assignment unit, an inline warning explains the inherited-assignment rule and links to methodology

Empty & Edge States

  • No qualifying flights (e.g., extreme filter combination): Empty state with "No flights match the current filter" and a link to browse the catalog
  • Insufficient sample per variant: Lifts hidden with a "Sample too small for this window" message; base counts still visible
  • SRM detected: Header banner; lifts still render but with a warning chip on every affected metric
  • Data stale: Banner with last-refreshed timestamp when SLA is exceeded
  • Loading: Skeleton state on charts while the flight join executes

Accessibility

  • Flight filter is keyboard-accessible; screen reader announces "Flight filter, current selection: X, Y variants available"
  • By-Variant view exposes numerical values as text alongside charts
  • Data-quality banners and inline warnings are announced with role="alert" or matching semantics
Section 6

Open Questions & Risks

Open Questions

Q1: What is the integration architecture for flight assignments in ClickHouse?

Kenny Long's comment on the deliverable — "large investment to bring in and join user-level data in CH to calculate metrics on the fly in RapidIQ" — is the biggest single open question. Three broad options:
(a) Join flight-assignment tables into ClickHouse at user grain and let RapidIQ query joined tables directly;
(b) Expose flight assignments as a queryable metadata source (dimension) that RapidIQ resolves at query time;
(c) A hybrid — assignments as a dimension, backed by CH user-grain joins for on-the-fly metric computation.
The choice drives cost, latency, and freshness. Owner: Engineering + IDEAS DS. Blocker for: dev estimate and funding decision.

Q2: What is the canonical source-of-truth for flight assignment history?

Need to confirm which IDEAS Flights / ExP dataset is the authoritative user-to-variant assignment record with the required history depth. Owner: IDEAS Flights team + Engineering.

Q3: How do we handle metric-grain vs assignment-unit mismatch?

Flights may be assigned at user grain but metrics may be defined at tenant or device grain. Need a documented inheritance rule (e.g., "assign a tenant to a variant if ≥N% of its users are in that variant") and a UX pattern for warning users. Owner: DS + PM.

Q4: What is the reconciliation tolerance vs ExP scorecards?

Variant counts and metric lifts will not necessarily match ExP scorecards to the row, especially given different windowing and stat frameworks. Need to define an acceptable tolerance and a public methodology page. Owner: DS.

Q5: Access & entitlement — does One Access already grant flight-level visibility?

One Access unifies portal access. We need to confirm whether flight-level access can be inherited from One Access + IDEAS Flights entitlements, or whether new authorization plumbing is required. Owner: Privacy + Engineering.

Q6: What is the acceptable freshness SLA?

≤24 hours is proposed; growth crews may want tighter (near-real-time) for ramp monitoring. Trade-off against integration cost. Owner: PM + Engineering.

Q7: How does this relate to Anytime-Valid Inference and Guardrail Decision Framework?

V1 exposes lifts and coarse CIs. Deeper stat rigor should align with the Anytime-Valid Inference and Guardrail Decision Framework specs. Need to define the boundary — RapidIQ as a lightweight lens, scorecards as the deciding artifact — so users don't misuse RapidIQ for decision-grade calls. Owner: PM + DS.

Risks

Risk 1: Integration Cost Blocks Funding (Large-Investment Signal)

Likelihood: High · Impact: High. The unresolved architecture question (Q1) is what drove the "not funded in Q1" status. Without a costed, phased plan, the deliverable will keep sliding. Mitigation: (1) Drive Q1 to resolution before requesting funding; (2) Phase the work: dimension-only (self-serve segmenting) as a cheap V1 slice, on-the-fly lift computation as a costlier V1.1 or V2; (3) Time-box a technical spike to characterize the CH-join cost.

Risk 2: Semantic Drift Between RapidIQ Lifts and ExP Scorecards

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: High. If numbers computed in RapidIQ diverge from scorecards, users will lose trust in both and default to bespoke pulls. Mitigation: (1) Publish reconciliation tolerance and methodology; (2) Automated reconciliation tests before GA and periodically after; (3) UX language explicitly frames RapidIQ as an exploration lens, scorecards as the decision surface.

Risk 3: Misinterpretation of Provisional-Metric Lifts as Decision-Grade

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: Medium. Provisional metrics are provisional by definition — self-serve access could invite premature "shipping on a provisional signal" decisions. Mitigation: (1) Tag provisional metrics with a visible "provisional — not scorecard-graded" chip in the By-Variant view; (2) Documentation on how to promote a provisional finding into a scorecard extension; (3) Reference the Guardrail Decision Framework for what qualifies as decision-grade.

Risk 4: Query Performance Regression Under Flight Joins

Likelihood: Medium · Impact: Medium. Adding user-level flight joins can regress dashboard render performance, particularly on high-cardinality flights or across large windows. Mitigation: (1) Perf budget defined in R-guardrail; (2) Pre-aggregation strategies (e.g., variant × cohort × day rollups) for common patterns; (3) Perf testing before GA; (4) Feature flag rollout with monitoring.

Risk 5: Access & Entitlement Complexity

Likelihood: Low–Medium · Impact: Medium. Flight-assignment data can expose information about who is in a treatment before the experiment is publicly discussed. Mitigation: (1) Reuse One Access + IDEAS Flights entitlements where possible; (2) Privacy review before GA; (3) Confidential-flight support (opt-in list, restricted visibility).

Risk 6: Displacing (Rather Than Complementing) Scorecards

Likelihood: Low · Impact: Medium. If self-serve segmenting is fast and flexible, teams may over-index on RapidIQ views instead of maintaining scorecard discipline. Mitigation: (1) Clear positioning: RapidIQ = exploration; scorecards + Expedite = decision; (2) Deep-links back to scorecards from any flight view; (3) Announcement framing that reinforces the boundary.
