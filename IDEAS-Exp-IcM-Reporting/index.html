Section 1

Problem & Opportunity

Problem Statement

IDEAS growth leadership can't see how long it takes to resolve operational incidents for scorecards, or how many incidents are being logged. Without that, leadership has no single view of which incidents are high priority, which are still open, and which need attention. Engineering also has no shared history of incidents to use when deciding where to spend effort.

Opportunity

An internal, leadership-only report on incidents owned by the OGA Experimentation IcM team would show the following:

  • Incidents logged, by severity
  • What's still open, and what has been open the longest without mitigation
  • Average time to mitigate over the last 28 days
  • A set of standard operational metrics: detection, mitigation, and resolution time; open count and aging; incident rate by service; automation coverage; auto-IcM generation rate; and SLA adherence

Because the customer is the person who submitted the IcM, the same data shows leadership who our customers are and who relies on our products most. It also gives engineering an incident history to prioritize against.

Evidence

  • PM observation: IDEAS growth leadership doesn't currently know how long incidents take to resolve or how many are logged.
  • Existing reports: IDEAS has no existing IcM report to build on (PM). The closest reference is the AQUA team's EM Operations Dashboard mock, which uses mock data.
  • Incident volumes, resolution times, customer counts: TBD No baseline data has been pulled for this spec yet.

Goals & Success Metrics

Goals

  1. At a glance, leadership can see how many high-priority (Sev 0-1) incidents are being worked on and how that has trended over the last 28 days.
  2. Make incident volume, open incidents, and time to mitigate visible to IDEAS leadership.
  3. Show who our customers are (IcM submitters) and how many incidents each one files.
  4. Give engineering an incident history it can use to prioritize work.

Section 3

User Scenarios

Scenario 1: IDEAS growth leader (primary)

A leader opens the report before an operations review. The top row shows how many Sev 0-1 incidents are open and being worked on, average time to mitigate over the last 28 days, and the 28-day trend. Below that, open incidents are listed by severity, starting with the ones that have been open longest without mitigation. The leader has what they need for the review without asking engineering for a status update.

Scenario 2: Leader asking "who are our customers?"

A leader wants to know who depends on the experimentation platform most. The customer view groups incidents by submitter, showing how many each one filed and at what severity. That shows which customers rely on our products and feel it most when something breaks.

Scenario 3: Engineering prioritization

An engineering lead reviews the incident history: incident rate by service, detection and mitigation times, and what share of incidents were raised automatically versus filed by hand. They use it to pick the services and failure modes most worth investing in.

Edge case: No open Sev 0-1 incidents

The Sev 0-1 tile shows 0, and the open-incidents table says "No open incidents" instead of being blank. The 28-day trend and average time to mitigate still show.

Edge case: Incident with no human submitter

Some incidents are created by monitors, alerts, or synthetics, not by a person. The reference mock's query treats these source types as auto-detected (source: reference mock). These incidents are counted under auto-IcM generation. The customer view should label them "Auto-generated" rather than attribute them to a person.

→ 28-day trend by severity → "Needs attention" list (by severity, longest without mitigation first) → click incident ID to open it in IcM

Figma: TBD

Key Interaction Notes

  • Everything in the KPI header has to be readable at a glance. That's the PM's main success criterion.
  • Incident IDs link to the incident in IcM. The report itself is read-only.
  • Severity is shown as text ("Sev 1"), not just a color.

Edge Cases & Empty States

  • No open incidents: Show "No open incidents" instead of an empty table.
  • Metric with no agreed definition: Show "Definition pending" instead of a number (see R-07).
  • Data refresh failed: Keep the last good data and show the last-refreshed time with a warning.
  • Auto-generated incident: Show it as "Auto-generated" in the customer view.

Accessibility

  • All tables and tiles can be navigated by keyboard. Incident links can be focused and open with Enter.
  • Severity and SLA status always have text labels, so color is never the only signal.
  • Trend charts have a table alternative for screen readers.
Section 6

Open Questions & Risks

Open Questions

Q1: What is the exact IcM owning-team value?

The team is "OGA Experimentation". The PM's IcM query identifies it as service 35021, team 115361 (source: IcM query). Still open: the matching OwningTeamName / team ID filter in the Kusto Incidents table, and whether any other teams under service 35021 are in scope. Owner: kumarvp (Eng).

Q2: How is "IDEAS leadership only" enforced?

Which security group or allow-list controls access, and who maintains it? Owner: TBD.

Q3: What does "being worked on" mean for Sev 0-1?

Does it mean open with an owner assigned, acknowledged, or simply any open incident? The reference mock tracks acknowledgement through OwningContactAlias. Owner: David Lydston (PM).

Q4: How is automation coverage defined?

It's on the PM's metric list but doesn't have a definition yet. It might mean auto-detection, auto-mitigation, or something else. Owner: David Lydston (PM) and kumarvp (Eng).

Q5: Which IcM field holds the submitter, and should submitters be grouped by team?

The customer is the person who submitted the IcM (PM). We still need the exact field, and to decide whether to show individuals or roll them up to their team or org. Owner: kumarvp (Eng).

Q6: How far back should engineering's incident history go?

The leadership metrics use a 28-day window. Engineering's history view needs its own window. For comparison, the reference mock uses 90 days for retrospectives and repair items. Owner: kumarvp (Eng).

Q7: How does data get from IcM into the app, and how often?

The reference mock notes that a browser app can't query Kusto directly; real data needs a backend API or an export (source: reference mock). Which approach will we use, and how often will it refresh? Owner: kumarvp (Eng).

Q8: What are the SLA targets for OGA Experimentation?

We need SLA adherence targets for TTD, TTM, and TTO. The mock's values are another team's targets, so they're reference only. Owner: TBD.

Q9: Should the retrospectives and repair items sections be included?

The reference mock tracks both for Sev 0-2 incidents. They aren't in the PM's requirements yet. Owner: David Lydston (PM).

Risks

Risk 1: The submitter isn't always the customer

Likelihood / Impact: TBD. Monitors create some incidents, and DRIs file others on a customer's behalf, so counting by submitter could misidentify customers. Proposed mitigations: keep auto-generated incidents out of the customer view, and check the submitter field against a sample of incidents.

Risk 2: Incident details reach people outside leadership

Likelihood / Impact: TBD. Incident titles and owners can be sensitive. Proposed mitigations: enforce access on the server side (R-01), and have the backend read only the IcM fields the report needs.

Risk 3: A few long incidents skew the average TTM

Likelihood / Impact: TBD. One or two very long incidents can pull the 28-day average far from what's typical. Proposed mitigation: show the median and the incident count next to the average.

Risk 4: Tight timeline

Likelihood / Impact: TBD. The target is October 2026, and several definitions are still open (Q1–Q8). Proposed mitigation: ship the P0 leadership view first and follow with the P1 customer and history views.
